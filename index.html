Collaborative care, done right, for Tennessee patients

A partnership with BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee

Over two years, the Institute for Integrated Behavioral Health ran a Collaborative Care Model pilot in an ETSU pediatric clinic in partnership with BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee (BCBST). BCBST supported the pilot to learn how CoCM and its billing codes work in Tennessee primary care, and is helping share what we learned with providers.

BCBST covers the CoCM codes, and a 2025 Tennessee law requires all third-party payers to reimburse them. Coverage helps patients only when the model is delivered as designed, which is what this page is for.

Billing for the CoCM codes

CoCM is billed once per calendar month, based on the total time the care team spends on a patient’s collaborative care across all contacts.

Getting it right

  • Track each patient’s CoCM time per calendar month as it happens.
  • Document the activities behind the time: outreach, follow-up, registry updates, and caseload review with the psychiatric consultant.
  • Confirm eligible billing practitioners and limits on billing other services in the same month.
  • Before you scale, send 8–10 sample claims to BCBST for feedback.

G2214 applies when the base-code time isn’t reached; verify payer rules. Last reviewed November 2026. Coding and payment rules change, so confirm current requirements with BCBST and CMS before billing.

Planning for sustainability

Expected revenue and break-even volume help you plan staffing:

Expected revenue = Billable service volume × Expected payment per service

Break-even volume = Total program cost ÷ Average net revenue per billable episode

Adjust for payer mix, denials, and collection rates. Costs include care manager and psychiatric consultant time, admin support, registry or technology, and training.

Implementing a registry

The registry keeps every enrolled patient in view so the team can spot those who aren’t improving. It doesn’t have to be fancy, but it has to be used.

At minimum, a registry should

  • List every patient enrolled in CoCM.
  • Track measures (such as the PHQ-9 or GAD-7) and follow-up over time.
  • Flag patients who disengage or aren’t improving as expected.
  • Support caseload review with the psychiatric consultant.

In our pilot

We built the registry in REDCap during an EHR transition. It tracked CoCM time, follow-up, psychiatric consultation, safety planning, and measures including the PHQ-A and GAD-7.

Recording CoCM time in the registry shows when a patient crosses a billing threshold for the month.

Compare registry options
Spreadsheet (secure Excel): low cost and easy to start; manual entry gets harder as volume grows.
Custom database (REDCap): highly customizable; needs local setup, maintenance, and access management.
Purpose-built CoCM registry (AIMS Caseload Tracker): designed for caseload review; may sit outside the EHR.
EHR-integrated (Epic or another EHR): fits existing workflows; depends on local informatics support.

Engaging psychiatry

The psychiatric consultant reviews the whole caseload with the care manager instead of seeing every patient, so one psychiatrist’s expertise reaches many more people. The primary care clinician (PCP) stays responsible for care and prescribing.

Plan these before you start

  1. Consultant: expertise that fits your population, with protected time.
  2. Rhythm: a predictable meeting schedule and format.
  3. Priorities: use the registry to bring forward new patients and those not improving.
  4. Follow-through: how recommendations are documented and reach the PCP.
  5. Coverage: who reviews cases when the consultant is away.

In our pilot

A psychiatrist with child and adolescent expertise met weekly with the behavioral health care manager (BHCM) for a one-hour, HIPAA-compliant video review. Physicians joined to present their patients when they could.

Common questions

Which conditions should we start with?

Start small. Many practices begin with depression, using the PHQ-9 to measure progress, and expand once workflows are solid.

Can follow-up happen by phone or telehealth?

Yes, and regular contact with the care manager drives improvement. Use only the channels your organization approves.

What training does the team need?

Everyone should learn CoCM workflows. Care managers typically complete AIMS Center training; our pilot also used the Meadows Collaborative Care Training Hub.

How will we know it’s working?

Track how many eligible patients are screened, referred, and enrolled, and follow each patient’s symptom scores over time, not just the average.
